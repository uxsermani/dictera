Build a lesson once. Update it everywhere. Except quizzes.

Today, fixing a typo in forty course sections means doing it forty times. CreateSpace is D2L's answer — one master copy, pushed out and kept current everywhere. It works well for pages, assignments and lesson folders. It does not work for quizzes at all. This briefing shows exactly where that line falls.

Everything here is checkable. Every fact comes from D2L's published documentation or public announcements, and every source is linked so you can open it yourself.

Where D2L contradicts itself, we say so rather than pick a side. One such conflict is live on their site right now.

02The basic idea

A shared cupboard for course content

Imagine every lesson and handout your team builds lives in one shared cupboard instead of being locked inside individual courses. You build it once, send copies out, and later improvements reach every copy. D2L describes it as a place where instructional design teams create, organise and manage reusable content before deploying it to Brightspace.

A

The problems it targets

D2L's own framing of the pain points.

Building safelyA dedicated space to create, away from live courses with students in them.
Working as a teamCentral teams build and manage material together instead of in isolation.
Escaping silosContent can be used across many courses rather than trapped in one.
Distributing at scaleMaster courses that get copied out to many sections.
B

How it sits with D2L's other tools

A common point of confusion, so worth being explicit.

H5PDeeply interactive content: games, interactive video, drag-and-drop.

CreateSpace does not replace Creator+

Creator+ builds what's on the page. CreateSpace manages where that page goes and keeps it current. Teams will use both.

03CreateSpace / Ecosystem

CreateSpace and its D2L relationships

CreateSpace sits within the broader D2L product ecosystem. The available evidence shows different levels of connection between CreateSpace and other D2L products, ranging from established relationships to areas that require further validation.

D2L Brightspace

The host platform. CreateSpace is reached from the Brightspace navbar or Admin Tools, and content built in CreateSpace is deployed into Brightspace courses. As of July 2026 CreateSpace is part of core Brightspace at no additional cost.

Confirmed · Documented

D2L Lumi Pro

D2L's roadmap states that authors will be able to use Lumi Pro inside CreateSpace to generate module content from uploaded files and modify pages in the Content Library. Announced for the November–January window, not available today.

On roadmap · Nov–Jan

D2L Creator+

A direct, documented relationship. D2L's Content Library documentation states that a CreateSpace Page is an HTML content page where you can use Creator+ elements. D2L also describes CreateSpace as "inspired and informed by our work on Creator+", and the original beta was open only to Creator+ customers.

Confirmed · Documented
"Free" comes with an asterisk — and this is a correction

Our earlier draft, and the previous version of this diagram, showed Creator+ as having no stated relationship to CreateSpace. That was drawn from D2L's marketing pages, which genuinely don't mention one. The technical documentation does: a CreateSpace Page is explicitly described as a page where Creator+ elements can be used.

The practical consequence matters. CreateSpace itself is now free, but Creator+ remains a paid add-on. So the container is free while the richer page-building tools inside it — accordions, tabs, callouts, flip cards — still depend on a Creator+ licence. Any budget conversation that treats "CreateSpace is included" as the whole picture will be wrong.

Worth confirming with D2L: whether Creator+ elements inside CreateSpace draw on the same licence and seat count as Creator+ elsewhere in Brightspace. We could not establish this from published documentation, and it is a direct cost question.

A note on lock-in. Analyst Phil Hill points out this idea isn't new — systems like Australia's HarvestRoad Hive attempted the same central content repository model in the 2000s, and in his assessment those ideas were ahead of their time and did not become established systems. CreateSpace revives the concept but makes it part of the LMS itself. As he puts it, on one hand this simplifies the technology landscape, but on the other it creates more dependence on a single vendor. That trade-off is worth naming for stakeholders — it is not on its own a reason to avoid the tool.

There is no single "on" button. Access is built from two system-wide switches plus eight individual permissions. If one is missing, nothing breaks loudly — a library simply doesn't appear, and nobody knows why. These are D2L's exact wordings, so your Brightspace administrator can match them.

Someone can change a course they don't teach

D2L states that with the appropriate administrative permissions, you can deploy content to any organizational unit even if you are not enrolled in the course. This is intentional — it is how a central team distributes content — but it makes "Can Sync and Deploy" a permission to hand out deliberately and narrowly.

Tell instructors their courses can change

The fourth permission decides whether an instructor is notified when someone else's update lands in their course. It is separate from the permission the person doing the update needs. Skip it and faculty will discover changes by stumbling across them.

One detail for your audit procedures

If an administrator uses Brightspace's impersonate-a-user feature, they can access CreateSpace when the impersonated user has the right permissions. The impersonated person's initials show in the navigation bar.

Quiz

Assessments with questions and automatic marking. You can build one and send it. You cannot update it — and how it fails is the point.

BuildWorks

What you can't do yet

D2L is explicit: quiz synchronisation is not supported, and updating a lesson folder that contains a quiz completes with a partial failure because the quiz is not synchronised.

Why this is the most important limit here

It isn't that quizzes don't update. It's that a folder containing one will half-update — everything else refreshes, the quiz quietly doesn't. And the notification won't say which part failed, because D2L confirms notifications don't identify what changed. The person doing the update sees a message, assumes it worked, and has no way inside the product to find out.

Recommendation

Keep quizzes in folders of their own, or out of CreateSpace entirely, until quiz updating ships. Mixing them into content folders turns every update into a silent half-success.

File types and size limits, for the Asset Library

Video: .mp4, .mov, .wmv — max 2 GB, output capped at 720p. Audio: .mp3, .m4a, .ogg, .wav, .wma — max 1 GB. Images: .jpg, .jpeg, .png. Documents: Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, OpenDocument. SCORM: .zip.

The 720p cap is a product limit, not a setting. If any UMGC programme depends on high-detail video — lab demonstrations, close-up technique, fine on-screen text — check this against current media standards before moving video in.

06Behaviour / what actually happens

These changes land in courses that already have students

This is the section to read twice. Everything before it is about building things. This is about what happens when your change reaches a live course in the middle of a term.

01

Two different actions

Send and Update are not the same thing.

Send (Deploy)Puts a copy into a course. Mainly used for lesson folders. If the target course has no folder, one is created.
Update (Sync)Pushes later changes to courses that already hold a copy. The option only appears for content already sent — otherwise it isn't in the menu at all.
02

What gets overwritten

And, just as importantly, what doesn't.

OverwrittenLeft alone
TitlesDue and availability dates
Page contentGrade items
InstructionsRubrics
Anything editable in CreateSpaceQuiz restrictions, instructor settings
Student progress is safe

D2L confirms that updating preserves learner progress. Completion and progress data are not reset when content is refreshed.

03

The surprise: mystery folders

Brief faculty on this before anything else.

If an update finds material in a course that isn't in your master copy, it doesn't delete it. Instead, the system creates a new folder in that course and moves the unmatched material into it for the instructor to review.

This happens in two situations: you deleted something from the master copy and then ran an update, or an instructor added their own material to the folder in their course and then an update ran. Nothing is lost, and the instructor is told to review it.

Every instructor who personalises a shared folder will get a mystery folder

The behaviour is deliberately safe — nothing is destroyed. But at UMGC's scale, one update across a large course could create these in dozens of sections at once, each needing an instructor to sort it out. This is the single most likely source of confused support tickets. Brief faculty before the first real update, not after.

CreateSpace does the hard part well. The gap is specific and identifiable, which makes it something to plan around rather than a reason to hold back.

Questions to put to D2L

Given the co-development relationship, these should get real answers.

  1. Do discussions update or not? Two of your live help pages disagree with each other.
  2. Is quiz updating on any roadmap window, and when?
  3. Will notifications ever say what actually changed?
  4. Can "Can Sync and Deploy" be scoped to parts of the organisation, or is unrestricted access the permanent design?
  5. What is the confidence level on the December 2026 date, given the previous one moved by a year?
  6. CreateSpace is now included at no cost, but Creator+ is not. Do Creator+ elements used inside CreateSpace draw on the same licence and seat count as Creator+ elsewhere in Brightspace?

Suggested approach — start with non-assessment content. Pages and assignments are well supported and update cleanly. Build the first wave on those, keep quizzes out of shared folders entirely, brief faculty about mystery folders in advance, and reassess at the December release using the five questions above as the checkpoint.
